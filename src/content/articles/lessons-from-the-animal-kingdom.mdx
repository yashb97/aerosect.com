Imagine watching the world with the kind of curiosity we often lose as we grow older. A bird lifts from a branch without a runway, a fish turns through water as if the river briefly forgot to resist, a butterfly balances on the wind like it has struck some private agreement with the air, and a housefly lands upside down on glass as though gravity is only a suggestion. These moments feel ordinary because they happen all around us, every day, but hidden inside them are answers to questions engineers are still learning how to ask. Biomimicry begins with that quiet astonishment: perhaps nature is not just around us, but ahead of us, casually solving problems we still need laboratories to understand.

What exactly is it about nature that always keeps us a few steps behind? Perhaps it is the self sustaining process of evolution that enables nature's constant adaptation to our environment (whether natural, or man made). We all know about how we, as humans evolved from monkeys, but I think that's just scratching the surface. Fair enough, we know where we come from to an extent, but what does it mean? What can we learn from that? Why does that happen in the first place? Also, that was probably nature reacting to nature, what about nature reacting to how humans have changed things in our environment? Let's take a deeper dive into some these examples, and see what we can learn from them.
